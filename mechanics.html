Core Rules & Systems

RPG System

To play Exalted, you will need roughly 2~3 people beyond yourself. One of them will be the Storyteller. The Storyteller acts as the eyes, ears, and primary antagonist of the world. While the players each control a single Exalted champion, directing their actions and speaking their words, the Storyteller controls everything else. They narrate the consequences of the players' choices, portray the sprawling cast of gods, mortals, and monsters, and set the stage for epic triumphs and tragic failures. The other participants are the Players, whose duty is to bring their individual Solars to life, working collaboratively to forge their legends and bend the destiny of Creation to their will.

Character Creation

The first step in creating your character is to think of a concept of who exactly your character will be. Remember, we're dealing with an age of antiquity, when cruelty was commonplace, and slavery was accepted, and drugs were common. You are a Solar, a demigod, a warrior, a conqueror, a king-yet-to-be, and many will seek to extinguish the small spark that you are before you have the chance to become as glorious as the sun above.

Once you have a firm grasp on your mortal background and the traumatic, glorious moment of your Exaltation, you will translate this concept into numbers on your character sheet. You must first select a Caste—the specific role the Unconquered Sun chose for you. You might be a fearsome Dawn Caste warrior, a zealous Zenith Caste priest-king, a brilliant Twilight Caste scholar, a shadowy Night Caste assassin, or an Eclipse Caste diplomat who binds oaths with magic.

Next, you will allocate dots to your Attributes (your raw physical, social, and mental capabilities) and Abilities (your learned skills, ranging from Melee and Bureaucracy to Occult and Stealth).

Playing the Game

The narrative of Exalted flows through a shared conversation, structured much like an epic play or a cinematic film. Time is primarily broken down into Scenes, which are distinct periods of continuous action in a single location. A scene could be a tense negotiation in a local warlord's silk-draped tent, a desperate chase across the rooftops of a crumbling city, or a bloody, mud-soaked skirmish against the walking dead.

Within these scenes, the core of the experience is Roleplaying. You describe what your character does, deliver their speeches, and make decisions driven by their grand ambitions and fatal flaws. Simply stating that you'll attack is not enough, better you describe exactly how it will be done, and shout out a battle cry as you do so.

Don't be afraid to be lame. A few misses will make the cool moments even more striking.

When the outcome of an action is uncertain—when you try to strike a nimble foe, forge a masterpiece from scrap iron, or lie to a god—you will resolve the conflict through Dice Rolling. Exalted relies on a pool of ten-sided dice (d10s). To determine how many dice you roll for an action, you typically add your rating in a relevant Attribute to a relevant Ability. For example, lifting a heavy portcullis might require rolling a number of dice equal to your Strength plus your Athletics.

You roll that pool of dice, and every die that shows a 7, 8, 9, or 10 is considered a 'success'. A 10 is an exceptional result and counts as two successes. The Storyteller sets a difficulty (a specific number of successes required to accomplish the task). If you meet or exceed that difficulty, your Solar bends reality to their will and achieves the impossible. If you fail, the Storyteller narrates how the harsh world of Creation pushes back against your divine light.

The Dice Pool

Whenever a character attempts an action with a chance of failure, the player rolls a number of 10-sided dice (d10s). The size of this dice pool is determined by adding the relevant Attribute and Ability together.

Target Number and Successes

  • The standard target number is 7. Any die showing a 7, 8, 9, or 10 is considered a Success.
  • Rolling a 10 counts as two successes (except when rolling for damage, where it only counts as one).
  • The Storyteller assigns a Difficulty to the task (usually 1 for standard actions, higher for harder tasks). You must roll total successes equal to or greater than the difficulty to succeed.

Botches

If a roll generates no successes (not a single die rolls 7 or higher) and at least one die shows a 1, the roll is a Botch. The action fails disastrously, usually resulting in a severe complication for the character dictated by the Storyteller.

Willpower and Virtues

  • Willpower: A player can spend one point of Willpower before rolling to automatically secure one guaranteed success on a roll, or they can spend it to activate certain powerful magical effects.
  • Virtues: You may channel a Virtue (Compassion, Conviction, Temperance, or Valor) by spending a point of Willpower. This adds a number of dice to your roll equal to the Virtue's rating, provided the action firmly aligns with that Virtue's philosophy.

Stunts

Describing your action with cinematic flair earns you a Stunt bonus. Stunts add extra dice to your pool (1 to 3 dice depending on how evocative the description is or how much it incorporates the environment). Additionally, successfully executing a Stunt allows you to recover lost Willpower or Essence motes.
